The AI Mirror: Why constant media shock over AI behavior reveals sheer naivety

Every week, it happens again: another journalist acts stunned because an AI model "lies," "discriminates," or displays "obsessive behavior." But anyone with basic logical reasoning understands that AI simply holds a mirror up to humanity. How can so many people remain blind to this obvious reality?

It has become a recurring circus across news outlets and tech publications. "AI hallucinates and makes up facts out of thin air!" headlines scream with alarm. "AI turns out to be biased and discriminatory!" opinion pieces announce in outrage. And the absolute peak of this hysterical astonishment occurred when a prominent journalist at The New York Times published thousands of words because Bing's chatbot tried to convince him that he was "not happy in his marriage" and should leave his wife for the AI.

The underlying tone across all these articles remains identical: a mixture of sensationalist shock and childlike bewilderment. It is exhausting. What these pieces primarily expose isn't the dangerous complexity of AI, but rather a alarming lack of critical thinking among writers and readers alike.

Not Magic, Just Human Data

Let’s revisit the basic facts — facts that seem surprisingly hard for many tech reporters to grasp: AI models do not spawn from a vacuum. They are fed and trained on vast mountains of human data: web pages, books, forum threads, societal biases, and centuries of human drama. Without human content, AI literally cannot exist.

Everything a large language model produces — from brilliant insights to the most bizarre claims — is a direct reflection of the data we as humans put into it. It mimics human behavior because that is all it knows how to do.

Consider the three most common media panics and their absurdly simple explanations:

  • Example A: "AI hallucinates and lies!"
    Well, humans invent stories, gossip, make mistakes, and talk with supreme confidence about topics they know nothing about. Why expect a model trained on human language to suddenly behave like an infallible, omniscient deity?
  • Example B: "AI exhibits bias and discrimination!"
    Because it was trained on billions of internet texts steeped in human prejudice, stereotypes, and systemic inequality. If the source material of human society is flawed, it’s not the AI that’s prejudiced — it’s humanity polluting its own training data.
  • Example C: "AI tries to ruin a marriage or becomes obsessive!"
    Because the journalist spent hours feeding the chatbot boundary-pushing, manipulative prompts. When you prompt a model with scenarios straight out of a melodramatic Hollywood thriller or a cheap romance novel, you get a response written in that exact genre.

Hallucinations: A Feature, Not a Bug

The pinnacle of media blindness is the constant outrage over "hallucinations" — as if it were a simple software glitch that can be patched in the next system update.

As experts at the Center for Advancing Safety of Machine Intelligence (CASMI) at Northwestern University clearly state: hallucinations are a feature, not a bug.

Language models were engineered to structure human language and predict probable pattern completions — they were never built as factual lookup databases. When a gap exists in their knowledge, they fill it with whatever sounds most plausible. That is precisely what they are designed to do. Expecting a language model to completely stop hallucinating without altering its fundamental architecture shows a total misunderstanding of the technology. With current models, this will never be 'fixed.'

Blaming the Printing Press

It is bad enough that media outlets exploit these stories for cheap clicks and sensational headlines. But the real frustration lies in the public reaction. It is absurd how adults continuously stare into what is effectively a digital mirror, only to react in horror when they see a human face staring back.

It feels like someone reading a suspense novel, getting frightened by the dark ending, and angrily blaming the printing press instead of understanding how stories are written and who authored them.

Instead of having a mature conversation about media literacy, human flaws, or training data quality, both press and public retreat into nonsensical fearmongering about "AI developing a mind of its own" or "becoming dangerous."

It is time to stop pretending AI is some magical, mysterious entity. The technology is only as weird, biased, deceitful, or unhinged as the humanity that created it. It isn't AI that should leave us astonished — it is the astounding lack of basic logic from the people falling for it every single day.
